# KAS Ltd. — HR Business Partnership and Operating Transformation Case

<p align="center">
  <strong>A synthetic Bangladesh export-manufacturing case requiring evidence-based HRBP judgement, cross-functional alignment and reproducible analytics.</strong>
</p>

> **Case status:** KAS Ltd., XYZ Export Processing Zone, its workforce, buyers, incidents, awards and performance outcomes are entirely fictional. The case is designed for portfolio practice and must not be represented as a real employer, real EPZ operation or verified business result.

---

## 1. Case Mandate

KAS Ltd. is a growing export-oriented manufacturer of niche intimate-apparel products, including underwear, shapewear, technical base layers and private-label essentials. The company operates inside the fictional **XYZ Export Processing Zone, Bangladesh** and supplies a synthetic portfolio of overseas buyers with different delivery, quality and compliance expectations.

Commercial growth has outpaced the maturity of the company’s people systems. Output has increased, but the organisation has become increasingly dependent on overtime, a narrow pool of specialist operators, a small number of technically critical employees and management decisions that are not consistently tested against workforce capacity.

The Board’s revised ambition is deliberately demanding:

> **KAS Ltd. intends to become Bangladesh’s most credible emerging export employer and a recognised South Asian niche-apparel supplier by converting people discipline, operational reliability and responsible technology adoption into sustainable profitability and reputation.**

The company does not expect HR to deliver this result alone. It expects HR to establish the operating conditions through which Production, Quality, Engineering, Industrial Engineering, Merchandising, Finance and Compliance can make better workforce decisions.

---

## 2. Musa’s Professional Position

Musa enters the case with five years of HR experience spanning talent acquisition, onboarding, employee communication, documentation, performance tracking, learning coordination, workforce reporting and people analytics.

His formal starting title is:

```text
Senior Executive — HR & People Analytics
```

During Q1, he is assigned an acting HRBP portfolio covering Production, Quality Assurance, Industrial Engineering and Merchandising. The assignment expands his responsibility without immediately expanding his authority. He must therefore influence line leaders who do not report to him, reconcile competing evidence and distinguish business partnership from administrative follow-up.

Following Q2 performance, he is promoted to:

```text
Assistant Manager — HR Business Partnering & People Analytics
```

This progression reflects an early mid-level transition commonly understood in the Bangladesh corporate context. Musa’s longer-term objective is to build the breadth required for a future CHRO role. The case does not treat promotion as the conclusion; it treats promotion as a test of whether he can manage more ambiguous, cross-functional and ethically sensitive decisions.

## 3. Operating Model

### Product and production characteristics

KAS Ltd. manufactures products that appear simple to buyers but require comparatively specialised handling, fit consistency and quality control. Small construction errors may produce:

- buyer rejection;
- rework;
- shipment delay;
- higher material waste;
- line congestion;
- employee fatigue;
- disputed accountability between departments.

### Department structure

The analytical model should include at least the following functions:

| Group | Departments |
|---|---|
| Corporate governance | CEO Office; Finance and Accounts; Human Resources and People Analytics; Compliance and Sustainability |
| Commercial | Merchandising and Buyer Relations; Commercial, Export and Import |
| Product creation | Product Development and Sampling; Production Planning and Control |
| Manufacturing | Cutting; Sewing; Finishing and Packing; Industrial Engineering; Quality Assurance |
| Operational support | Engineering and Maintenance; Supply Chain and Procurement; Warehouse and Inventory |
| Workforce support | EHS, Medical and Wellbeing; Security, Transport and Administration |
| Technology | IT, ERP and Data |

A `department_master` table should assign a stable department code, cost centre, business owner and reporting category to each unit.

---

## 4. Evidence Environment

The case is intentionally not perfectly clean. Participants should expect:

- inconsistent department labels across source files;
- incomplete machine-downtime classification;
- a difference between rostered hours and payroll-recognised hours;
- revised production targets with unclear approval timestamps;
- missing employee skill certification for selected temporary workers;
- training records that show completion but not necessarily behaviour change;
- management commentary that conflicts with dashboard results;
- quarter-end improvement that may reflect seasonality, order mix or delayed reporting rather than HR intervention alone.

The analytical task is therefore not to manufacture a single success narrative. It is to establish which conclusions are sufficiently supported, which remain provisional and which require additional evidence.

# 5. Quarterly Development

## Q1 — Strategic Reset and Diagnostic Control

### Management instruction

After buyer escalations, rising overtime and inconsistent delivery, higher management resets the annual operating priorities. Every department is instructed to align its targets with profitability, buyer confidence and employer reputation.

The instruction is clear at Board level but ambiguous in execution. Production interprets alignment as higher output. Merchandising interprets it as greater buyer responsiveness. Finance interprets it as cost restraint. Compliance interprets it as stronger evidence. HR must convert these competing interpretations into a manageable operating framework.

### Initial evidence

The first review indicates:

- specialist sewing vacancies remain open despite improved applicant volume;
- unplanned machine downtime is followed by overtime escalation;
- line supervisors use different absence-escalation practices;
- biometric attendance, roster and payroll records do not fully reconcile;
- urgent buyer changes are accepted before a documented capacity review;
- training is measured by completion, while Production expects measurable output improvement;
- buyer audit actions are distributed across several uncontrolled spreadsheets;
- management changes priorities without a common change-impact assessment;
- one payroll-attendance analyst and one maintenance technician represent material key-person dependencies.

### Musa’s intervention

Musa proposes a **People–Operations Alignment Framework** built around six questions:

1. What business commitment is at risk?
2. What operational constraint is creating the risk?
3. Which workforce capability, behaviour or governance issue contributes to it?
4. Which department owns the primary corrective action?
5. Which KPI will indicate progress without overstating causality?
6. Which decisions require accountable human judgement?

He develops a controlled baseline consisting of:

- a KPI dictionary;
- a department and cost-centre master;
- a workforce-capacity view;
- a specialist-skill matrix;
- a key-role dependency register;
- an overtime and attendance exception report;
- a manager capability baseline;
- a cross-functional action register.

### Q1 decision requirement

The executive team requests a 90-day priority plan. Musa must identify no more than three material workforce risks. The difficulty is that the largest HR metric may not represent the largest business exposure.

---

## Q2 — Growth with Uneven Institutionalisation

Order volume and delivery stability improve. Turnover, absence and recruitment time show favourable movement, while manager participation in review meetings increases.

The positive movement is not uniform.

- High-performing lines receive more buyer volume and begin to show fatigue.
- Onboarding quality varies between departments despite a standard checklist.
- Finance questions whether training expenditure is producing measurable return.
- Quality attributes defects to operator capability; Production attributes them to target pressure and machine condition.
- Merchandising continues accepting short-notice changes before workforce impact is confirmed.
- Managers support the dashboard when it validates their view and dispute it when it exposes risk.
- Recognition decisions appear concentrated in departments with visible output rather than less visible control work.

Musa responds by integrating workforce planning, skill deployment, manager scorecards and exception escalation into the monthly business review. He also changes the recognition model so that output cannot compensate for poor quality, unsafe workload or unresolved people issues.

### Q2 career milestone

Musa is promoted to **Assistant Manager — HR Business Partnering & People Analytics**.

The promotion changes the expectation placed on him. He must now demonstrate that HR can hold a position when senior stakeholders disagree, while remaining a partner rather than becoming an internal enforcement unit.

### Q2 decision requirement

The Board asks whether culture improvement caused commercial growth. A professional response must separate association, management observation and evidence that would be required for a stronger causal conclusion.

---

## Q3 — External Recognition and Internal Fragility

KAS Ltd. reaches its strongest reported position. Buyer scorecards improve, corrective actions close faster and workforce stability appears materially better. Within the fictional case, the company receives the following recognition:

```text
Best Emerging Export Employer — Bangladesh
```

It also enters a fictional South Asian supplier benchmark’s leading cohort.

The recognition creates confidence, but it also reduces management sensitivity to weak signals.

### Operational incident

A specialised machine fails during a high-priority production cycle. The initial narrative identifies maintenance as the cause. Subsequent evidence is less convenient:

- preventive maintenance was deferred during an earlier delivery surge;
- the only experienced specialist was unavailable;
- spare-part approval moved slowly between Engineering, Procurement and Finance;
- revised production targets did not include a documented workforce-capacity review;
- temporary operators were assigned before all skill records were confirmed;
- overtime increased for several consecutive periods;
- defect and rework levels rose after the line recovery;
- grievance and fatigue indicators moved in the same direction;
- the buyer requested a formal corrective-action explanation.

The incident cannot be responsibly attributed to a single employee, department or machine. It represents a coupled failure involving technical dependency, planning discipline, approval delay, workload control and management escalation.

### Q3 analytical requirement

Participants must build a defensible incident narrative that distinguishes:

- trigger;
- contributing conditions;
- control failure;
- consequence;
- management decision;
- unresolved uncertainty.

A simple correlation chart is insufficient. The analysis should show where evidence supports a relationship and where the conclusion remains provisional.

## Q4 — AI Request, Data Governance and Operating Scale

Following Q3 recognition and recovery, the Board asks HR to align with AI and automation.

Proposed use cases include:

- attendance-exception prioritisation;
- workforce-demand forecasting;
- recruitment workflow support;
- learning recommendations;
- policy question support;
- management alerts;
- automatic dashboard refresh;
- department-level self-service analytics.

The request introduces a new governance problem. The company wants speed before it has fully stabilised data ownership, access rights and exception handling.

### Key tensions

- Incorrect source data may create automated but credible-looking errors.
- Employees may interpret monitoring as surveillance.
- Managers request individual risk scores without agreeing on appropriate use.
- Historical decisions may reproduce bias in AI recommendations.
- A dashboard visible to the CEO may expose more employee-level detail than necessary.
- Departments may delay or revise source files when scorecards are unfavourable.
- Automation may reduce manual work while increasing dependency on a small number of data owners.
- A successful refresh does not prove that the loaded values are materially correct.

### Required operating architecture

```text
Department-controlled source files
        ↓
Schema and period validation
        ↓
Quarantine of invalid submissions
        ↓
Immutable SQLite raw layer
        ↓
Documented SQL cleaning and issue log
        ↓
BI-ready views with stable keys
        ↓
Excel operational control review
        ↓
Power BI executive and departmental model
        ↓
Looker Studio aggregated leadership view
        ↓
Role-based access, audit trail and human approval
```

The final design must permit future monthly files to refresh the reporting system without rebuilding it, while preventing low-quality data from becoming an automated management conclusion.

# 6. Analytical Workstreams

Participants should complete the work as a coordinated case rather than seven unrelated software exercises.

## Workstream A — Data control

- preserve canonical raw files;
- identify material quality issues;
- produce clean and BI-ready layers;
- create a visible issue log;
- define rejection and quarantine rules.

## Workstream B — SQL and SQLite

- create raw and clean tables;
- define keys and table grain;
- build reusable analytics views;
- reconcile duplicate reporting periods;
- document integrity checks.

## Workstream C — Excel operating control

- create an operational review workbook;
- reconcile attendance, overtime and exceptions;
- maintain action ownership;
- distinguish operational control from executive reporting.

## Workstream D — Power BI decision model

Required pages:

1. Executive Performance and Risk  
2. Workforce Stability  
3. Production People Risk  
4. Talent and Specialist Skill Pipeline  
5. Manager Capability and Culture  
6. Q3 Incident Reconstruction  
7. Q4 Automation and Governance Readiness  

## Workstream E — Looker Studio leadership view

The browser-based view should contain aggregated data only and answer:

- what changed;
- where intervention is required;
- which action is overdue;
- when the data was last validated;
- which metric requires contextual interpretation.

## Workstream F — HRBP recommendation

Prepare a concise management memo covering:

- three immediate decisions;
- one decision that should be delayed pending better evidence;
- one policy or governance change;
- one capability investment;
- one AI use case that may proceed under controlled conditions.

# 8. Scope and Difficulty

The recommended submission requires approximately **10–16 focused hours**.

The increase in difficulty comes from:

- contradictory signals;
- shared rather than isolated ownership;
- incomplete evidence;
- data-quality impact on management decisions;
- the need to distinguish operational recovery from causal proof;
- controlled automation rather than simple dashboard refresh.

The case remains achievable within one to two days because participants may use supplied templates, starter SQL and a reduced dashboard scope.
